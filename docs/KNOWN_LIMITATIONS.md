# Known Limitations

1. The bundled vocabulary bank contains 10,000 training cards. Their A1–C2 labels are curriculum decisions, not an official CEFR word-count table: CEFR provides no universal fixed vocabulary threshold, and the full deck has not been psychometrically calibrated against a certified learner population.
2. Placement uses a 60-item adaptive instrument (ten per band, of which most learners see six) and has not undergone psychometric norming against a large certified learner sample. Since 3.24 a band stops as soon as a Wilson interval at 80% confidence separates the learner from the 67% pass mark, and carries on to the reserve items when six could not. Where ten answers still straddle the mark the result says so rather than presenting the band as measured. Two things this does not fix: multiple-choice items cannot distinguish a confident learner from a lucky one at the margin, and the 80% operating point is a judgement about a low-stakes recommendation, not a calibrated error rate.
3. Listening uses synthesised German rather than studio-recorded multi-speaker audio. Since 3.9 native builds bundle a neural voice (Piper VITS, Thorsten, CC0) run on device through sherpa-onnx, so Android, desktop and Apple builds hear the same voice instead of whatever the operating system happens to have installed. The web build still uses a browser voice. It is a good narrator and still a single speaker: there is no dialogue between distinct voices, no regional accent and no spontaneous connected speech.
4. Writing evaluation is an offline transparent heuristic and cannot replace trained human scoring for advanced CEFR production.
5. **The speaking tutor is a scripted role-play, not a language model.** It follows a fixed sequence of authored turns and evaluates each reply with a deterministic rule engine. It will not improvise, will not answer a question you ask it, and will not follow you off-script. What it does reliably is tell you whether you addressed the turn, at sufficient length, using the target structures.
6. **Pronunciation scoring hears the sound, but does not identify phonemes.** Since 3.17 the lab records the learner and compares that audio with the same sentence rendered by the bundled voice, using MFCC features and dynamic time warping. That reaches timing, rhythm, stress and vowel shape, which the word-matching never could. It still cannot tell you that your /y/ came out as /u/ — only that a stretch sounded unlike the reference — and the reference is a synthesiser, so an accent a German listener would accept still costs points. The 0–100 scale is anchored on two measured figures (the same sentence re-synthesised at a different speed scores 12.4; a different German sentence scores 30.7) and on **no human recordings at all**, so where a pass mark belongs among real learners is not yet established. Where speech recognition exists, the text comparison still runs and is reported alongside, because it catches dropped and wrong words that an acoustic score cannot distinguish from bad pronunciation.
7. **Speech recognition is unavailable on Linux entirely.** `speech_to_text` has no Linux implementation, so there is no transcript there and typed answers remain the way to complete a speaking turn. Since 3.17 Linux is no longer without any spoken feedback, though: recording works, so the pronunciation lab scores the audio against the bundled voice. You get a pronunciation score without a transcript, which is the opposite trade from every other platform. Recording on Linux needs `pulseaudio-utils` and `ffmpeg`, which most desktops already have.
8. The iOS artifacts CI produces are **unsigned**. The release includes an `.ipa`-shaped archive and the raw `.app`, but neither carries a provisioning profile or trusted signature; putting either on a device needs re-signing with your own Apple identity.
9. Speech recognition quality is the platform's, not the app's. On Android the system recogniser may route audio to the vendor's servers unless a German offline language pack is installed; on some devices and emulators no recogniser exists at all. Every speaking screen therefore accepts typed input as an equal path.
10. Free-talk scoring checks, since 3.24, whether the vocabulary of each content point actually appears in the answer: every prompt carries authored German keyword sets, matched through a light stemmer that folds umlauts and inflectional endings, and coverage is 60% of the score. An off-topic answer of the right length now scores under 45 and is told which points it missed. What this still cannot do is comprehend: someone who names their sister without saying anything about her counts as having covered “who is in your family”, a bare thesis with no marker is detected only through its topic vocabulary, and a keyword used to deny a point counts the same as one used to make it. It detects the vocabulary of a point, not the point.
11. Stories are original graded texts written for this app, not authentic published literature. They are level-controlled rather than natural.
12. The SM-2 implementation uses conventional parameters rather than parameters fitted to this deck's review logs; it is a sound default, not a tuned model. **It cannot currently be tuned at all**: only aggregate per-item state is stored (`WordProgress`, `ActivityProgress`), never individual review events, so there is no history a modern scheduler such as FSRS could be fitted against. Adding a bounded append-only review log is the prerequisite for any scheduler work, and would also enable honest retention statistics and an undo for a misgrade.
13. Intervals are spread by up to five per cent since 3.24, so a session's cards no longer all come due on the same later day. The spread applies from three days upward; shorter intervals are left alone because moving a one-day interval by a day is a different schedule rather than a spread. The grade buttons deliberately preview the unspread number. What is still true is that the *parameters* underneath -- the SM-2 ease steps -- remain untuned, for the reason in #12.
14. Achievements, quests and streaks are local. There is no leaderboard, no social feature and no server, so nothing here is comparable across users. There is also no cloud sync: moving a profile between platforms is a manual export/import, and the two copies diverge from the moment you continue studying on both.
15. Exam-prep mini mocks train task types and strategy but are not full-length official provider papers.
16. Real exam formats can change. Verify official provider pages before registration or final preparation.
