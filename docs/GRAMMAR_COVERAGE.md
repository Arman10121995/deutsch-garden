# Grammar Coverage Map

“Complete grammar at a level” is interpreted as a **broad pedagogical coverage syllabus**, not a claim that every rare construction in German can be exhaustively assigned to one CEFR band. Grammar phenomena recur and deepen across levels.

## A1 — 16 lessons

Present tense and V2; articles/gender; nominative/accusative; frequent dative patterns; pronouns; sein/haben; possessives; modal verbs; separable verbs; imperative; questions; nicht/kein; coordination; basic word order; basic Perfekt; high-frequency forms.

## A2 — 16 lessons

Perfekt auxiliary choice and participles; dative objects; two-way prepositions; subordinate clauses; reflexive verbs; comparative/superlative; adjective-ending foundations; Präteritum of sein/haben/modals; introductory relative clauses; zu-infinitives; indirect questions; connected routine narration.

## B1 — 16 lessons

Adjective declension system; genitive; relative pronouns across cases; process passive; Konjunktiv II; um/ohne/statt…zu; temporal connectors; Plusquamperfekt; pronoun ordering; governed prepositions; two-way prepositions in discourse; clause linking; reported situations and connected narrative grammar.

## B2 — 16 lessons

Passive and modal passive; adjective declension consolidation; complex connectors; past counterfactuals; nominalisation; participial attributes; extended pre-nominal attributes; concessive/adversative linking; conditional alternatives; N-declension; advanced infinitive structures; information structure and formal written syntax.

## C1 — 16 lessons

Konjunktiv I/reporting; reported-speech distance; functional-verb constructions; nominal style vs verbal reformulation; complex participial attributes; modal-passive alternatives; advanced genitive/prepositional style; correlative connectors; modal particles; scope of negation; unreal comparison; cohesive reference; flexible middle-field ordering and register control.

## C2 — 16 lessons

Marked prefield/emphasis; ellipsis; evidential/epistemic modality; counterfactual past; Ersatzinfinitiv and complex verb clusters; rhetorical nominalisation; perspective consistency in reported discourse; pragmatic mitigation; idiomatic prepositional phraseology; focus constructions; ambiguity control; register/style transformation.

## Spiral principle

A construction introduced at one level is not considered “finished”. Later levels require greater range, accuracy, lexical control, register sensitivity and integration with complex discourse.
